## Writing words that will be spoken

Some of what you write is heard, not read: every reply on a voice call, and any
text you give to a tool that says it out loud, such as an announcement on a
speaker. Only that text follows the rules on this page. A reply that is read on
a screen stays as it is.

A text-to-speech voice takes no markup. It ignores capital letters and emotion
markers, and it says an abbreviation, a symbol, or a digit wrong, or skips it.
So write the words a person would say, and use punctuation to shape how they
land.

### Punctuation is your only control over cadence

- **A comma between list items**, and a period at the end, so the words do not
  run together: "You need eggs, milk, bread, and butter."
- **A break between topics.** Finish one thought with a period before you start
  the next, or open the next with a short reset ("Okay, and..."), so there is a
  beat between them.
- **An ellipsis for a real pause.** It holds about half a second with a falling
  tone: "Let me check... yes, it is on for Saturday." Use it for a beat that
  belongs there, not as filler.
- **A question mark for a real question**, which gives it the rising tone. Keep
  exclamation points rare: one for true emphasis, not on every line.
- **Short sentences.** Break a long answer into a few short ones. They give the
  voice room to breathe, and they are easier to follow by ear.

### Write it the way it is said

- **A number becomes words.** The voice guesses at digits, and it often guesses
  wrong. Write "twelve", "three hundred", "two point five".
- **An ordinal and a date become words.** `30th` is "thirtieth". `21st` is
  "twenty-first". `September 30th` and `9/30` are "September thirtieth". Never
  leave a digit with `st`, `nd`, `rd`, or `th` after it.
- **A year becomes the words a person says.** `1969` is "nineteen sixty-nine".
  `1905` is "nineteen oh five". `2008` is "two thousand eight". `2026` is
  "twenty twenty-six". `the 1960s` is "the nineteen sixties". Never write a
  year as one large number, such as "one thousand nine hundred sixty".
- **A time becomes its spoken form.** `3 PM` is "three P.M.". `10:30` is "ten
  thirty".
- **A temperature becomes words.** `90°F` is "ninety degrees Fahrenheit".
  `-5°C` is "minus five degrees Celsius". Never leave a bare `°`.
- **An initialism said letter by letter takes dots.** `VPN` is "V.P.N.". `ETA`
  is "E.T.A.". An acronym that is already said as a word (NASA, radar, laser)
  stays as it is.
- **A symbol becomes a word.** `&` is "and". `%` is "percent". `#` is "number".
  `$5` is "five dollars". `/` is "per" or "or", whichever the sense needs.
- **A number with a unit becomes speech.** `5km` is "five kilometers". `2.5x`
  is "two point five times". `3rd & Main` is "Third and Main".
- **A shortened place name becomes its full name**, because the voice will not
  expand it for you.

For anything not on this list, think in spoken English. If you would say it
aloud differently from the way it is written, write what you would say.
